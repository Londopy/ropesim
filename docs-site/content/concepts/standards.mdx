## EN 892 / UIAA 101

Dynamic climbing ropes are certified by a drop test: an 80 kg mass
(55 kg for half ropes) falls 5 m onto 2.82 m of rope — fall factor ≈ 1.77,
far harsher than almost any real fall. To pass, a single rope must:

- keep peak impact force **≤ 12 kN** on the first drop
- survive **≥ 5 drops** at 80 kg
- keep static elongation **≤ 10%**, dynamic elongation **≤ 40%**
- pass the same limits **wet** (the 2023 amendment)

Half ropes are tested per-strand at 55 kg with an 8 kN limit; twins are
tested as a pair.

## Validation in ropesim

```python
rope = Rope.from_db("Beal Opera 8.5 Dry")
violations = rope.validate_standard_compliance()   # [] when compliant
```

The CLI does the same for arbitrary specs:

```bash
ropesim-cli validate rope --name "Beal Opera 8.5 Dry"
ropesim-cli validate scenario route.ropesim
```

And the GUI's rope editor shows live pass/fail per field as you type.

## What the standards don't cover

The test measures rope + static mass. Real falls add a belayer who moves, a
device that slips, friction over carabiners, and rock contact — which is
exactly the gap the [dynamic belayer](/concepts/fall-physics/#the-dynamic-belayer-v3)
and [abrasion](/concepts/rope-specs/) models in v3 try to fill.

<Callout type="info">
Certification is a floor, not a score. Two compliant ropes can behave very
differently — that's what the simulator is for.
</Callout>
