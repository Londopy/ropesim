Fall physics

The UIAA fall factor model, explained from first principles.

The fall factor#

The severity of a climbing fall is governed not by how far you fall, but by the ratio of fall distance to the rope available to absorb it:

fall factor  f = fall_distance / rope_out

Fall 4 m on 8 m of rope and f = 0.5. Fall 4 m on 2 m of rope — say, straight onto the belay with no protection — and f = 2.0, the theoretical maximum and the geometry the EN 892 drop test approximates (5 m fall on 2.82 m of rope, f ≈ 1.77).

ropesim clamps the factor to [0, 2] and rejects rope_out <= 0:

from ropesim._rustcore import compute_fall_factor
compute_fall_factor(5.0, 2.82)   # 1.773

Peak impact force#

Treat the rope as a spring and conserve energy through the catch:

F_peak = mg + sqrt( (mg)² + 2·mg·f·k )

where k is the length-normalised stiffness (kN). Rope length cancels out — force depends only on the fall factor. This is the key insight behind the whole UIAA framework: a 2 m fall near the belay can hurt more than a 20 m whipper high on the pitch.

ropesim back-calculates k for each rope from its published EN 892 impact force, so database ropes reproduce their spec-sheet numbers exactly under test conditions.

The force-time curve#

The closed-form peak assumes an undamped spring. For the actual force history ropesim integrates a damped spring ODE with RK4 in Rust:

dδ/dt = v
dv/dt = g − (kδ + cv)/m     while the rope is taut

with damping from the dimensionless ratio ζ (typically 0.05–0.25 for dynamic ropes). The integration runs at your chosen timestep until the rope goes slack after the first bounce.

result = fall.simulate()
curve = fall.force_curve_numpy()   # kN per timestep

Modifiers#

| Modifier | Effect | Source | | --- | --- | --- | | Wet rope | +12% peak force | EN 892 wet test allowance | | Temperature | stiffer below 20 °C, +2%/°C below 0 °C | Leech (2002), UIAA | | Belay device | friction fraction absorbed, 0.33 (tube) – 0.70 (I-Device) | device test data | | Rope wear | up to +20% stiffness at end of rated falls | nylon fatigue data | | Dynamic belayer | ×0.55–1.0 force reduction | Pavier (1998) — see below |

The dynamic belayer (v3)#

A belayer who gets lifted — or deliberately jumps into the catch — absorbs real energy. v3 models this explicitly from the climber/belayer mass ratio, stance, device slip, and technique:

from ropesim import dynamic_belay_reduction

# 55 kg belayer, 80 kg climber, tube device, deliberate soft catch:
dynamic_belay_reduction(80, 55, "atc", soft_catch_technique=True)  # ≈ 0.66

A soft catch can cut peak force by 30–45% for a light belayer — often the difference between slamming into the wall and a gentle swing.
